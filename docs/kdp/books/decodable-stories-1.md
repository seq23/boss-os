# For the grown-up: how to use this book

This book is for a child who knows some letter sounds and is starting to blend them into words. Each story uses only the sounds taught in that story and the stories before it, plus a few tricky words to learn by sight. That means your child can sound out almost every word on the page. That is what "decodable" means.

## How the stories build

- **Story 1** uses m, s, a, t, p, n and c.
- **Story 2** adds i, d, g, h and b.
- **Story 3** adds o, f, l and r.
- **Story 4** adds e, k, w and j.
- **Story 5** adds u, v, y and z.
- **Story 6** adds sh, ck, ll and ss.
- **Story 7** adds ch and th.

Many classrooms teach the sounds in a similar order, but not all do. Start with the first story that uses only sounds your child already knows, and go back if it feels hard.

## Before each story

Each story opens with two short lists:

- **Sounds in this story.** Point to each one and ask your child to say its sound, not its letter name. For "m", the sound is "mmm", not "em".
- **Tricky words.** Words like "the" and "said" do not follow the sounds your child knows yet. Read them together and practise them by sight before the story.

## While you read

- Let your child point to each word as they read it.
- When they get stuck, ask them to say each sound and then blend them: "c... a... t... cat."
- Do not guess from the pictures or the first letter. The point is to use the sounds.
- An ending s can sound like "s" (cats) or "z" (digs). Both are fine.
- Praise the effort: "You sounded that out all by yourself."
- Keep it short. One story a day, read twice, is plenty.

## After each story

Each story ends with two questions for you to ask. Talking about the story builds understanding as well as reading.

## Reading it again

Rereading is not cheating. The second and third read are where reading becomes smooth. Many children love reading a story they have mastered to someone new: a grandparent, a pet, a favorite toy.

This book is reading practice. It is not a reading program, a test, or a treatment for any learning difference. If you are worried about your child's reading, talk to their teacher or doctor.

# Story 1: A Cat Nap

## Sounds in this story

- m
- s
- a
- t
- p
- n
- c

## Tricky words

- the
- a

## Practice words

- Sam
- Pam
- sat
- cat
- pat
- nap
- tap
- pan
- can
- tan

## A Cat Nap

Sam sat.

A tan cat sat.

Sam can pat the cat.

Pat, pat, pat.

The cat can nap.

Sam can nap.

Pam can tap a pan.

Tap, tap, tap.

The cat naps.

Sam naps.

Pam sat.

A cat nap.

## Talk about it

- What did Pam do with the pan?
- Do you think the cat liked to nap? Why?

Ted gets his red pen and his kit.

Ted is set.

## Bud and the Bus

Bud is a pup.

Bud and Gus sit in the sun.

"Yes, the bus," said Gus.

Gus runs to the bus.

Gus gets on the bus.

Bud sat up.

Gus left his cap.

Bud gets the cap.

Bud runs and runs.

## The Fish Shop

Bess and Dad will shop.

The fish shop is up the hill.

In the shop, fish swim in a big tub.

Bess spots a fish.

It is red and black.

"Can I get it, Dad?" said Bess.

"Yes," said Dad.

The man in the shop gets a net.

## Chip and the Chick

Chip sits on a bench in the sun.

Chip spots a chick in the grass.

The chick is thin and wet.

The chick is lost.

Chip picks up the chick.

"Peck, peck," went the chick.

Chip checks the hen pen.

Ten chicks sit with the hen.

"This chick is lost," said Chip.

"Can you help it?"

The hen clucks.

Cluck, cluck.

Chip sets the chick in the pen.

The chick runs to the hen.

Then the chicks and the hen nap in a big bunch.

Chip sits on the bench.

That was a lot of fun.

## Talk about it

- How did Chip know where the chick lived?
- How do you think the chick felt at the end?

# What comes next

Once your child can read all seven stories smoothly, they are ready for the next sounds: blends at the ends of words, the long vowel sounds, and more tricky words. Book 2 in this series picks up from here.

Until then, reread the stories, and try writing the practice words together: say a word, and let your child write the sounds they hear.
