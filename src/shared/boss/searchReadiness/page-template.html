Search Readiness Checklist

The seven switches that were off

Each of these was found on a live site in October 2026, after months of content work. None shows up in a Lighthouse score. All seven are now checklist items below, marked BURNED.

1 · Links

Backlinks marked nofollow pass nothing

The three library sites sent 341 links into the portfolio, every one tagged rel="nofollow sponsored". That tag is the instruction "do not count this link". The backlink network had been running for months at zero effect.

Check: look at the rel attribute on every link you are counting on. Only paid links and links to sites you do not vouch for should carry nofollow or sponsored.

2 · Indexing

A noindex tag on the public page

aplayermode.com shipped with <meta name="robots" content="noindex">, added to keep it out of an internal registry. It also kept it out of Google. Robots, sitemap and llms files all redirected to the homepage as well.

Check: curl -sL site/ | grep -i robots on every public page type, and fetch /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml, /llms.txt expecting 200 and the right content type.

3 · Canonical

Canonical pointing at a site you don't own

A client site's every page declared a canonical on a Webador site from an earlier era. Google credited that site and ignored this one: 112 pages, 4 impressions a month. Search Console rejected the sitemap because its URLs were on the other host.

Check: rel="canonical" on every page is the page's own URL on the one host you chose; www redirects to it; sitemap URLs match it.

4 · URLs

Ranked URLs returning 404 after a restructure

virtualagency-os.com moved pages under /programmatic/ and left the old URLs dead. 149 of them had been ranking and carried 72% of the site's impressions. Traffic fell to 26 impressions a month and nobody noticed because nothing measured it.

Check: every URL Search Console has ever shown must answer 200 or 301. Generate redirects from the Search Console export on every build and fail the build if a measured URL would 404.

5 · Demand

Hundreds of pages for searches nobody makes

Content programs were writing to 120 and 240 "target queries" taken from keyword tools. After six months, zero of those queries had ever appeared in Search Console for any site. Google had crawled the pages and declined to index most of them as thin or duplicate.

Check: a target query must have a measured number (Search Console impressions, or a measured volume). No number, no page. Strengthen the pages Google already shows at positions 5 to 15 before writing anything new.

6 · Sitemap

A sitemap that says every page changed today

Daily build cycles rewrote every page's lastmod to today, every day, on 115 and 323-page sites. Google learns within weeks that a sitemap like that is noise and stops trusting it for crawl scheduling.

Check: lastmod comes from a content hash of the page's main content, not the build date. A rebuild with no content change keeps the old date. Pin it with a test.

7 · Reach

Impressions landing on privacy and terms pages

approvalprep.com's few impressions were on its legal pages, and eight guides were in the sitemap but reachable by no link on the site. Google had nothing better to show.

Check: noindex the legal and utility pages; every content page reachable within three clicks from the homepage; no sitemap URL that no page links to.

The checklist

A lead-gen or hyperlocal vertical

Added 13 October 2026 while building the sixth local-guides vertical (a Memphis senior-care guide that routes family leads to facilities). Each item was a gap found in the first brief, not in the sites. The pattern: a canonical site that holds the guides and the intake, a companion citation site that routes to it, a directory built from a public registry, and a lead funnel that is measured next to the search numbers.

Launch order for a new site

  1. Host decisions first: one canonical domain, www redirect, HTTPS, the Search Console domain property verified and the service account added. Do this before the first page exists.
  2. Template before content: title, description, H1, definition slot, FAQ slot, social tags, the JSON-LD graph, icons, canonical. Every page inherits them; nothing is retrofitted.
  3. Crawl files: robots.txt, sitemap with content-derived lastmod, llms.txt, 404 page. Validators for all of them, proven negatively.
  4. Measurement wired before launch: daily Search Console collection, daily coverage, Clarity, CTA refs with a readable token, IndexNow key and ping, sitemap submission in the cycle.
  5. Launch with 5 to 15 strong pages, not 115 thin ones. Each answers a measured query with a definition, an FAQ and specific facts.
  6. For a lead-gen vertical: the canonical site ships its thick guides, FAQ page and intake first, from the measured shape of an existing vertical; the directory comes from the public registry with contact coverage measured; the companion citation site routes to the canonical intake at every turn; the lead funnel is in the weekly report before the first lead (phase J).
  7. Google Business Profile on day one for anything local; phone, photos, category, hours; the Maps link on the site.
  8. Links: followed, contextual, capped, disclosed; aimed at the pages you are strengthening.
  9. Week 2 review: coverage counts, first queries, which pages Google shows. Strengthen those; freeze everything else.
  10. Week 6 review: consolidate what Google declined; rebuild the target set from what it showed; write new pages only for measured queries.

Doing all of it for $0

Every item on this page can be run at no monthly cost. The portfolio spent $98.84 on model calls between August and October 2026 and nothing paid produced a measurable result; the three things that moved numbers (removing a noindex, fixing a canonical, following the backlinks) cost nothing. On 11 October 2026 every repo was moved to the posture below and the key that paid for the calls was capped at $0 a month, so a mistake cannot spend.

Setting it up in a new repo in one pass

  1. Copy the three templates from existing repos: the router module (how-we-know/loop/llm_router.py and its test), the daily Search Console workflow (dream-wedding-builder/.github/workflows/search-intelligence.yml, the search measurement loop), and the Monday report with discovery (local-guides-citation-velocity/.github/workflows/search-outcomes-weekly.yml).
  2. Create the OpenRouter key named after the repo, set its monthly limit to $0 in the dashboard, add it as the repo's Actions secret. Add GSC_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON and the Resend key the same way.
  3. Run the daily workflow by hand once and check the three data files land. Run the Monday workflow once and check the email and the receipt.
  4. Break each guard once: put a paid model in the config, watch the test fail, restore. Dispatch the probe without the flag, read the named stop.
  5. Then start the content jobs. Every one reads measured targets, routes through the router, and leaves a receipt with cost 0.

SEO, AEO, GEO in one line each

SEO (search engine optimisation): getting indexed and ranked in Google and Bing results. Host, crawl, on-page, links. AEO (answer engine optimisation): being the extracted answer in featured snippets, "People also ask" and voice assistants. Definition paragraphs, question headings, snippet-length answers, lists and tables, FAQ markup. GEO (generative engine optimisation): being cited by AI answer engines (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Claude). Entity clarity in structured data, llms.txt, AI crawlers allowed, specific sourced facts, a named author and organisation. The three overlap almost entirely; a page built for one serves the others.
